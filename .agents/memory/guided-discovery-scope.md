---
name: Guided discovery scope
description: User-stated simplicity and interaction requirements for the public career discovery journey.
---

Keep the homepage simple, retaining “Your next chapter,” without the working-day CTA, work-style/place feature cards, gateway section, or Overview/Portals header links.

After the quiz, use one primary Continue action on result screens. Put the personality spectrum above “Who walks your path,” with three role models in one row and their details revealed only on selection. Career recommendations should be visual and concise. CV collection should feel like a game/dialogue, one question at a time with choices or typing as appropriate, before login.

In the post-quiz conversation, single-choice answers advance immediately without another Continue click. Let users go back and revise earlier answers without losing them. Multiple selections and typed responses still need an explicit confirmation. Keep prompts short and reveal secondary explanations only when expanded.

Use an iOS-like glass treatment for wording, buttons, dropdown fields, and the personality spectrum tubes while preserving readable contrast.

**Why:** The user explicitly rejected the crowded homepage, competing result-page actions, text-heavy career recommendations, form-like CV intake and having to click Continue after every single-choice answer.

**How to apply:** Preserve this linear interaction model when extending public discovery; do not add more top-level choices or ask for login before the CV conversation.
